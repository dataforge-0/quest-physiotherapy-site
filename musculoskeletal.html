Musculoskeletal & Orthopedic Physiotherapy

Our name for treating pain and injury in your muscles, joints, and bones. Find what you're dealing with below, and we'll walk you through it.

What can we help with?

What it feels like

A dull, constant ache, sharp pain when you bend or twist, stiffness first thing in the morning, or — with sciatica — pain, tingling, or numbness that travels from your lower back down through your buttock and leg.

What usually causes it

Awkward lifting, long hours sitting or at a desk, sleeping in a poor position, muscle strain, or a disc irritation. Sometimes it comes on suddenly; sometimes it builds up gradually over weeks without one clear trigger.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist conducts a comprehensive musculoskeletal assessment — a structured evaluation of spinal mobility, neurological function, and load tolerance, including reflex testing and strength screening where nerve involvement is suspected (in plain terms: checking whether a nerve is being irritated or pinched). You'll leave with a clear clinical impression and an evidence-based treatment plan.

How treatment helps

Care combines hands-on manual therapy — joint mobilization and soft tissue release — with a progressive, biomechanically-informed exercise program designed to offload irritated structures, restore functional range of motion, and rebuild the neuromuscular control that prevents pain from returning.

What to expect: Many people notice real improvement within 3–4 visits; a full course of care is often 5–10 sessions, depending on how long you've had the pain and how severe it is.

What it feels like

Pain when lifting your arm, reaching overhead or behind your back, or lying on that side at night. With frozen shoulder specifically, the joint gradually stiffens until everyday movements — reaching for a seatbelt, doing up a bra — become genuinely difficult.

What usually causes it

Overuse from repetitive overhead activity, a rotator cuff strain, general wear from age, or — with frozen shoulder — no obvious cause at all; it can develop on its own over months.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist performs a detailed shoulder assessment — testing passive and active range of motion, rotator cuff strength through specific orthopedic tests, and scapular mechanics — to differentiate between rotator cuff pathology, adhesive capsulitis (the clinical term for frozen shoulder), and other structural causes.

How treatment helps

Care progresses through manual joint mobilization to restore capsular mobility, combined with a graded strengthening protocol targeting the rotator cuff and scapular stabilizers — the muscle group most responsible for long-term shoulder function.

What to expect: A rotator cuff strain often improves over 4–8 sessions; frozen shoulder is a slower process, sometimes several months, since the joint itself needs time to loosen.

What it feels like

Swelling, bruising, sharp pain at the moment of injury, and a joint or muscle that feels weak, unstable, or painful to put weight on — a rolled ankle, a pulled hamstring, or elbow pain from repetitive swinging or gripping.

What usually causes it

A sudden twist or awkward landing, overuse from repetitive motion in a sport, or jumping back into activity without enough warm-up or conditioning.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist conducts a sport-specific biomechanical assessment — testing ligamentous stability, strength deficits, and movement patterns — to grade the injury's severity and identify anything that warrants further imaging or referral.

How treatment helps

Rehabilitation follows a structured, phase-based return-to-sport protocol — progressing from pain and inflammation management through strength, proprioceptive (balance and joint-awareness), and sport-specific movement retraining — to minimize re-injury risk.

What to expect: Minor sprains and strains often resolve in 2–6 weeks; more significant injuries can take longer, with a structured return-to-sport plan along the way.

What it feels like

Stiffness, swelling, and weakness around the surgical site — normal after a hip or knee replacement or other orthopedic surgery — along with hesitation about how much movement is actually safe.

What usually brings people in

A surgeon's referral for post-operative rehab, or simply wanting guided, professional support to regain strength and confidence rather than navigating recovery alone.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist reviews your surgical report and surgeon's protocol, then conducts a baseline functional assessment — range of motion, strength, and gait analysis — to establish your starting point and a staged rehabilitation timeline.

How treatment helps

Rehabilitation follows an evidence-based, criterion-driven progression — advancing from range-of-motion and swelling management through progressive resistance training — timed to your tissue healing stage, not a generic calendar.

What to expect: Recovery timelines vary widely by procedure — a typical joint replacement rehab plan runs several months, with regular sessions in the earlier weeks tapering as strength returns.

What it feels like

Joint stiffness that's often worse in the morning or after sitting a while, aching that builds through the day, and a gradual sense that movements you used to do easily now take more effort.

What usually causes it

Osteoarthritis (wear-related joint changes over time) is the most common cause; rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory conditions are less common but treated differently, so an accurate assessment matters.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist evaluates joint range of motion, functional capacity, and movement compensation patterns across the affected joints, using this clinical picture to prioritize the activities and movements that matter most to your daily life.

How treatment helps

Treatment applies evidence-based exercise therapy — targeted strengthening and joint-friendly movement retraining shown to reduce pain and improve function in osteoarthritis and inflammatory joint conditions — alongside manual techniques to manage stiffness.

What to expect: This is usually an ongoing plan rather than a fixed number of sessions, adjusted over time — the goal is steady, manageable improvement you can maintain long-term.

What it feels like

Neck pain and stiffness, headaches, and reduced range of motion — symptoms that sometimes don't show up until a day or two after the accident, which surprises a lot of people.

What usually causes it

The sudden back-and-forth motion of your neck during a collision, which strains muscles and ligaments even at relatively low impact speeds.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist performs a cervical spine assessment — range of motion, neurological screening, and evaluation of associated symptoms such as cervicogenic headache or dizziness — with findings documented to support your HCAI claim.

How treatment helps

Treatment combines manual therapy with a progressive cervical stabilization program, addressing both acute symptoms and the underlying strength deficits that whiplash-associated disorders commonly leave behind. Your claim is billed directly through HCAI — see our direct billing page for how that works.

What to expect: Mild whiplash often improves within a few weeks; more significant injuries can take longer, and your treatment plan is documented to support your claim throughout.

What it feels like

Pain or strain from a specific incident — a lift, a fall, a sudden movement — or the slower build-up of repetitive strain from tasks you do every shift.

What usually causes it

Manual lifting, repetitive motion, awkward postures sustained over a shift, or a one-time accident on the job.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist completes a functional capacity-oriented assessment, evaluating the specific physical demands of your role alongside your current impairment, and documents findings in the format required for your WSIB claim.

How treatment helps

Treatment is structured around a graduated return-to-work protocol, combining manual therapy and targeted strengthening with functional task simulation, so your recovery is measured against the actual demands of your job. WSIB is billed directly — see our direct billing page for details.

What to expect: Your treatment plan and progress are documented throughout, in the format your WSIB claim requires, so recovery and paperwork move forward together.

What it feels like

Jaw pain or clicking when chewing or opening wide, tension headaches, and pain around the temples or ears — sometimes linked to clenching or grinding your teeth, especially at night.

What usually causes it

Jaw clenching or grinding (often stress-related), poor neck posture, or dysfunction in the jaw joint itself, which is more closely linked to your neck than most people realize.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist assesses temporomandibular joint (TMJ) mechanics, cervical spine mobility, and muscular tension patterns — since jaw and headache symptoms are frequently linked to upper cervical dysfunction — to build an accurate clinical picture.

How treatment helps

Treatment combines manual therapy targeting the jaw and upper cervical spine with a targeted exercise and habit-modification program to reduce clenching-related tension at its source.

What to expect: Many people notice less tension and fewer headaches within a handful of sessions, though habits like clenching take ongoing awareness to fully settle.

What it feels like

Unsteadiness, spinning (vertigo), or a general nervousness about falling — especially when turning your head, standing up quickly, or walking on uneven ground.

What usually causes it

Inner ear issues, general deconditioning from inactivity, or lingering effects from a previous fall, injury, or concussion.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist performs a vestibular and balance-oriented assessment, including positional testing and dynamic balance measures, to determine whether your symptoms originate from the inner ear, the musculoskeletal system, or a combination.

How treatment helps

Treatment applies vestibular rehabilitation therapy — a specific, evidence-based exercise protocol shown to retrain the balance system — alongside strength and confidence-building exercises that reduce fall risk.

What to expect: Balance-related dizziness often improves significantly within a few weeks of consistent, guided exercise.

What it feels like

Nagging, low-grade aches that don't point to one clear injury — stiffness after sitting, soreness after normal activity, or a general sense your body doesn't move the way it used to.

What usually causes it

A sedentary routine, gradual muscle deconditioning, repetitive daily movement patterns, or a minor issue that never quite got addressed before it became a persistent one.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist conducts a whole-body movement screen — assessing posture, load distribution, and compensatory movement patterns — to identify the underlying mechanical driver rather than treating the symptomatic area in isolation.

How treatment helps

Treatment addresses the root biomechanical cause through a corrective exercise program, targeting the specific mobility and strength deficits identified in your assessment.

What to expect: Most people feel a noticeable difference within a few sessions, with a home program that keeps things improving between visits.

What it feels like

Pain around the hip, groin, or the back of the pelvis (often described as pain over the dimples above your buttocks), sometimes worse with walking, standing on one leg, or rolling over in bed.

What usually causes it

Sacroiliac (SI) joint irritation, hip joint issues, pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain, or muscle imbalances around the hip and core.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist performs a differentiated assessment of the hip joint, sacroiliac joint, and surrounding musculature using specific orthopedic provocation tests, to accurately localize the source of your pain — since hip and pelvic pain can originate from several different structures.

How treatment helps

Treatment targets the specific structure identified — hands-on joint mobilization, core and hip stabilization exercises, or a combination. Where pain relates to pregnancy or the pelvic floor specifically, your physiotherapist will point you toward our dedicated pelvic floor physiotherapy care.

What to expect: Many people notice improvement within 4–6 sessions; pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain often responds well to targeted support and movement strategies throughout the remainder of pregnancy.
