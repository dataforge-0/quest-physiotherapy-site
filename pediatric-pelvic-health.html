Pediatric Pelvic Health

A gentle, age-appropriate approach to bladder and bowel concerns in children, handled with the same one-on-one care as our adult pelvic floor practice. Find what you're noticing below, and we'll walk you through it.

What can we help with?

What you might notice

Your child continues to wet the bed regularly past the age where dryness is typically expected, despite otherwise normal daytime bladder control.

What usually causes it

Often a combination of bladder-bowel coordination, deep sleep patterns, and pelvic floor muscle function — rarely a sign of anything more serious, though worth assessing properly.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist takes a gentle, age-appropriate history with you and your child, and assesses pelvic floor and bladder-bowel coordination through simple, non-invasive means suited to children.

How treatment helps

Treatment focuses on bladder retraining strategies, pelvic floor coordination exercises made into games, and practical routines for you to reinforce at home.

What to expect: Many families see improvement over a period of weeks to months with consistent home practice alongside sessions.

What you might notice

Your child has occasional or frequent daytime urinary accidents beyond the age where this is typically expected, sometimes alongside urgency or a rush to the bathroom.

What usually causes it

Pelvic floor muscles that aren't coordinating properly with the bladder, sometimes alongside constipation, which can put added pressure on the bladder.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist assesses bladder habits, toileting posture, and pelvic floor coordination in a comfortable, child-friendly way.

How treatment helps

Treatment includes bladder and toileting habit retraining, playful pelvic floor coordination exercises, and practical strategies for school and home routines.

What to expect: Most families notice steady improvement over a period of consistent practice, typically weeks to a few months.

What you might notice

Your child strains, avoids using the toilet, has infrequent bowel movements, or has bladder accidents that seem connected to bowel habits.

What usually causes it

Constipation and bladder dysfunction are closely linked in children — a full bowel can press on the bladder and disrupt normal bladder signaling and control.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist assesses toileting habits, posture, and pelvic floor coordination, working alongside any guidance from your child's doctor regarding diet and bowel management.

How treatment helps

Treatment combines toileting posture and technique coaching, pelvic floor coordination exercises, and practical, family-friendly routines to support consistent bowel habits.

What to expect: Improvement often follows consistent routine changes over several weeks, alongside physiotherapy support.
