Pediatric Physiotherapy

Play-based assessment and treatment for infants and children, with guidance you can carry home between visits. Find what you're noticing below, and we'll walk you through it.

What can we help with?

What you might notice

Your baby consistently tilts or turns their head to one side, has a flattened spot on one side of the head, or seems to prefer looking in one direction only.

What usually causes it

Tight neck muscles on one side (torticollis), often related to positioning in the womb or shortly after birth, which can lead to a flat spot developing from consistent pressure on one side of the head (plagiocephaly).

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist performs a full pediatric assessment — measuring neck range of motion, muscle tightness, and head shape — and builds a play-based treatment plan suited to your baby's age and stage.

How treatment helps

Gentle, hands-on stretching and positioning techniques, paired with specific play-based exercises you'll learn to continue at home, gradually restore neck mobility and reduce pressure on the flattened area.

What to expect: Early intervention — ideally starting before 6 months — tends to see the fastest results, often within a small number of sessions plus consistent home practice; older infants may need a longer course of care.

What you might notice

Your child isn't rolling, sitting, crawling, or walking within the general expected timeframes, or their movement looks different from what you'd expect.

What usually causes it

A wide range of factors — some children simply develop at their own pace, while others benefit from targeted support to build strength, coordination, or confidence in movement.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist conducts a developmental movement assessment against established pediatric milestones, identifying specific strength, balance, or coordination gaps in a comfortable, play-based setting.

How treatment helps

Treatment uses play-based, developmentally appropriate exercises — disguised as games, not "therapy" — to build the specific skills your child needs, with guidance for you to continue between visits.

What to expect: Progress is tracked against developmental milestones, with many families seeing steady gains over a period of weeks to months, depending on your child's starting point.

What you might notice

Your child walks on their toes rather than flat-footed, or their feet turn inward or outward more than expected when they walk or run.

What usually causes it

Often a habit or muscle tightness with no underlying concern, though in some cases it relates to muscle tone or coordination that benefits from targeted support.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist assesses gait pattern, muscle flexibility, and lower limb alignment, to distinguish a typical developmental variation from something that would benefit from a structured treatment plan.

How treatment helps

Play-based stretching and strengthening exercises, along with gait retraining activities, help your child build the flexibility and awareness to walk in a more typical pattern.

What to expect: Many children respond well within a handful of months of consistent, playful practice.

What you might notice

Your child seems clumsier than peers, struggles with activities like catching a ball, riding a bike, or navigating stairs, or tires quickly during physical play.

What usually causes it

Underlying differences in motor planning, balance, or muscle strength — sometimes on their own, sometimes alongside a broader developmental picture.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist assesses balance, coordination, and motor planning through age-appropriate movement tasks, to understand exactly where your child could use support.

How treatment helps

A structured, play-based program targets the specific coordination and balance skills identified, building both physical capability and your child's confidence in their own body.

What to expect: Progress varies by child; many families see improved confidence and capability within a few months of regular sessions.

What you might notice

Your child has had a fracture, sprain, or surgical procedure, and needs support regaining strength, movement, and confidence in the affected area.

When this comes up

Sports injuries, falls, or a surgical procedure requiring structured rehabilitation to get back to normal activity safely.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist reviews any medical or surgical history and any guidelines from your child's doctor, then builds an age-appropriate, play-based rehabilitation plan.

How treatment helps

A gradual, playful progression of movement and strengthening activities rebuilds function safely, at a pace matched to your child's healing and comfort.

What to expect: Timelines vary by injury; your physiotherapist will give you a clear sense of the expected course after the first assessment.
