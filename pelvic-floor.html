Pelvic Floor Physiotherapy

Assessment and treatment for bladder, bowel, and pelvic pain concerns, in a private room with an unhurried appointment. Find what you're dealing with below, and we'll walk you through it.

What can we help with?

What it feels like

Leaking urine when you cough, sneeze, laugh, exercise, or lift something; a sudden, strong urge to go that's hard to control; or needing to use the bathroom more often than feels normal.

What usually causes it

Weakened or uncoordinated pelvic floor muscles, often related to pregnancy, childbirth, menopause, chronic straining, or high-impact activity over time.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist completes a comprehensive pelvic health assessment — a detailed history followed by an external, and where appropriate, internal pelvic floor muscle examination assessing tone, strength, and coordination. Every step is explained beforehand, and internal assessment only proceeds with your full, informed consent, which you're free to withdraw at any time.

How treatment helps

Treatment applies individualized pelvic floor muscle retraining — targeted strengthening or relaxation techniques depending on whether your muscles are weak or overactive — combined with bladder retraining strategies to reduce urgency and leakage.

What to expect: Many people notice meaningful improvement within 6–8 sessions, though full pelvic floor rehabilitation is often a longer, gradual process.

What it feels like

Aches through pregnancy — pelvic, back, or hip — or after delivery: weakness, heaviness, scar sensitivity (C-section or perineal), or simply not feeling like your body has "come back" the way you expected.

What usually causes it

The physical demands of pregnancy and delivery on the pelvic floor, abdominal wall, and surrounding structures — vaginal and C-section delivery both bring recovery needs, just different ones.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist conducts a comprehensive postpartum assessment — evaluating abdominal wall integrity, pelvic floor function, and scar tissue mobility where relevant — to build a recovery plan matched to your delivery history and current stage.

How treatment helps

A progressive, evidence-informed rehabilitation program addresses core and pelvic floor coordination, scar tissue mobilization where needed, and a safe, structured return to exercise and daily activity.

What to expect: Most people benefit from starting around 6+ weeks postpartum, or as cleared by your doctor, with a program that evolves over several months as your body rebuilds strength.

What it feels like

Pain in the pelvic region, lower abdomen, or during intercourse — sometimes sharp, sometimes a persistent ache, often accompanied by muscle tension you may not have realized was there.

What usually causes it

Overactive or tight pelvic floor muscles, scar tissue, nerve sensitivity, or a combination of physical and stress-related factors.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist takes a detailed, sensitive history and performs an external assessment, with an internal examination offered — never assumed — when clinically appropriate and only with your explicit, ongoing consent.

How treatment helps

Treatment often includes manual therapy to release muscular tension, relaxation and breathing techniques, and graded exercises to reduce sensitivity and rebuild comfortable function, all paced to what feels manageable for you.

What to expect: This can be a gradual process; many people notice a meaningful reduction in pain over 8–12 sessions, with ongoing communication about pacing throughout.

What it feels like

A visible or palpable gap or doming along your abdominal midline, often noticed during pregnancy or postpartum, sometimes with a feeling of core weakness or lower back discomfort.

What usually causes it

The natural stretching of abdominal connective tissue during pregnancy, which doesn't always fully resolve on its own after delivery.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist measures the width and depth of the separation and assesses how well your deep core system — including your pelvic floor — is coordinating with it, since the two are closely linked.

How treatment helps

A targeted, progressive core-retraining program — quite different from generic ab exercises, which can worsen the issue — rebuilds tension and function in the connective tissue and surrounding muscles.

What to expect: Many people see meaningful improvement over 8–12 weeks of consistent, guided exercise.

What it feels like

A sensation of heaviness, pressure, or "something falling out" in the vaginal area, often worse by the end of the day or after standing or exercise.

What usually causes it

Weakening of the pelvic floor's support structures, commonly related to childbirth, chronic straining, or the natural effects of aging and menopause.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist assesses the degree and nature of your symptoms, and — with your consent — performs an internal examination to directly evaluate muscular support.

How treatment helps

Physiotherapy can meaningfully reduce prolapse-related symptoms through targeted pelvic floor strengthening and pressure-management strategies — safer lifting, breathing, and bracing techniques for daily life.

What to expect: Many people experience symptom improvement within 8–12 sessions; more advanced cases may be managed alongside your physician.

What it feels like

Straining, a feeling of incomplete emptying, or infrequent bowel movements that don't respond to dietary changes alone.

What usually causes it

Pelvic floor muscles that aren't coordinating properly during a bowel movement — either too tight or not engaging correctly — sometimes alongside dietary or lifestyle factors.

Your first visit

Your physiotherapist assesses pelvic floor coordination and toileting mechanics, and discusses your history and habits to identify what's actually driving your symptoms.

How treatment helps

Treatment includes muscle coordination retraining, toileting posture and technique guidance, and biofeedback-style exercises that improve the mind-muscle connection during a bowel movement.

What to expect: Many people notice improvement within 6–8 sessions, alongside any dietary adjustments discussed with your physician.
